Is ActiveCampaign Worth It in 2026? Read This Before Your Bill Doubles

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

ActiveCampaign has a pricing mechanic that catches a lot of people off guard, and it's not really about the sticker price on the plan page. It's about what happens to that price over the following twelve months — and by the time most users notice, they're already deep enough into the automations to feel stuck.

Worth it if: you'll actually use the automation depth and you model your cost at double your current contact count before committing.

Look elsewhere if: you're ecommerce-first (try Klaviyo) or your usage is closer to a monthly newsletter (try Brevo).

How the Pricing Actually Works

ActiveCampaign charges by contacts stored, not emails sent. That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. A list that grows from lead magnets, webinar signups, or an ecommerce store adding every checkout — even non-buyers — can double in a year without you sending a single extra campaign. The plan you signed up for at $15 or $49 a month quietly becomes a different number by your next renewal, and nothing about your actual usage changed.

This isn't a hidden fee in the sneaky sense — it's disclosed. But it's the kind of thing that's easy to model wrong at signup, because most people price based on where their list is today, not where it'll be in twelve months. ActiveCampaign force-migrated existing customers onto a new plan structure in 2024, and independent pricing trackers estimate the net effect has been roughly a 40% increase since then, with some customers reporting bills 30–40% higher once add-ons are included. That's the structural result of billing by total stored contacts — including unsubscribes and bounces — rather than by actual send volume, not a one-off billing mistake.

What You're Actually Paying For

To be fair to ActiveCampaign: the automation engine is the reason people put up with the pricing curve in the first place. Multi-branch workflows, deep CRM integration, and site-tracking-triggered sequences are genuinely more capable than most competitors at a comparable price point — which is exactly why switching feels painful once you're a year in and have twenty automations built.

The problem isn't that the product is bad. It's that the pricing model rewards you for staying small and penalizes you for succeeding.

Where It Falls Short

Only a 14-day trial. In a market where most serious competitors now offer a permanent free tier, forcing a purchase decision in two weeks is an outlier — and not in ActiveCampaign's favor.

The interface has a real learning curve. This isn't a "drag three blocks together" tool. Getting real value out of the automation builder takes time, and reviewers consistently flag the complexity as a barrier for non-technical users.

Add-ons compound the base price. CRM pipelines and SMS aren't included at every tier — stack them onto a mid-tier plan and the effective monthly cost can climb well past the advertised number.

Which Plan Fits, If You're Staying

Starter (~$15/mo at 1,000 contacts): fine for a simple newsletter and one or two automations — but you'll outgrow the automation limits fast if you're the type of user this tool is actually built for.

Plus (~$49/mo): the realistic floor once you're building real automation sequences and want CRM/SMS add-ons available. Most small businesses land here.

Professional (~$79/mo) and up: only worth it once you need the deeper behavioral segmentation and site-tracking features — don't upgrade preemptively.

Who Should Look Elsewhere

If your business is fundamentally an ecommerce store, Klaviyo or Omnisend are worth pricing out first — both are built around purchase behavior and revenue attribution in a way ActiveCampaign, as a horizontal tool, isn't. If you're early-stage and cost-sensitive, Brevo offers unlimited contacts on its free tier (a structurally different pricing model that avoids the exact problem described above), and Mailercloud undercuts ActiveCampaign's entry price while covering similar ground for simpler use cases.

The Verdict

ActiveCampaign earns its price if you're actually going to use the automation depth — multi-step sequences, CRM-linked triggers, behavioral segmentation — and you're disciplined about checking your contact count against your plan's ceiling before it becomes a surprise renewal. If your usage is closer to "send a monthly newsletter and a welcome sequence," you're paying for automation capacity you won't use, and a cheaper, simpler tool will serve you better.

Before you sign up — or before your next renewal — model your cost at roughly double your current contact count. If that number still makes sense for what you're getting, it's a solid tool. If it doesn't, it's worth trying one of the alternatives above before the bill decides for you.
